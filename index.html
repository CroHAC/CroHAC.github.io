CroHAC

Crowd-based Human-Agent Collaborative Software Engineering

Studying how diverse human and agent contributions become collective capabilities in software engineering.

About the workshop

How can the design of collective mechanisms promote the emergence of collective intelligence in software engineering?

CroHAC examines the rules that shape heterogeneous human-agent collectives: participant composition, task and contribution structure, information flows, incentives, aggregation, and feedback. It studies how complementary human knowledge and agent capabilities can produce collective problem-solving, judgment, and error correction.

A crowd is a distributed pool of contributors recruited through an open call or a participatory platform. The workshop connects this perspective with software engineering problems and the evaluation of collective mechanisms.

It brings together researchers, practitioners, tool builders, and students in software engineering, crowdsourcing, collective intelligence, human-computer interaction, and AI to develop evaluation principles and a shared research roadmap.

Research themes

Three connected areas structure the agenda across software development and testing.

Composition and contribution

Diversity and complementary expertise, crowd recruitment, incentives, participation rules, and the decomposition and recombination of software tasks and artifacts.

Information and aggregation

Information sharing, independence of judgments, preservation of disagreement and minority contributions, evidence aggregation, and collective review, testing, and error correction.

Feedback and evaluation

Adaptive feedback, learning in human-agent collectives, benchmarks and measures of collective gain, mechanism ablation, resource-aware evaluation, governance, and accountability.

Across the software lifecycle

Requirements, design, implementation, code review, verification, test generation, debugging, maintenance, and evolution.

Evaluating collective gain

Comparisons should use the same actors and resource budgets under alternative mechanisms, with individual, human-only, and agent-only baselines. Mechanism ablation helps identify which choices contribute to correctness, coverage, discovery, and robustness.
